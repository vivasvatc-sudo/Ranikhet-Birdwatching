Spotted Forktail

Enicurus maculatus
SPFOR
Resident
Year-round · easiest in winter
1,200–2,400m

A stream bird, and one of the most dependable good sightings on the route. Where the pheasants need luck and a pre-dawn climb, forktails work a fixed stretch of water and come back to the same boulders every morning.

When is the best time to see a Spotted Forktail near Ranikhet?

Year-round, though winter is easiest — birds drop to streams around 1,400–1,900m and concentrate on fewer stretches of water. Early morning and the last hour of light are best, when they feed along wet boulders. There is no narrow seasonal window here, which is why it is a good bird to build a trip around if your dates are fixed.

Where to look

Fast, shaded streams with exposed boulders and leaf litter along the edge. The Chaubatia stream below the orchards is the reliable site near Ranikhet; the crossings inside Binsar hold a pair or two. Walk the water slowly and stop often — they flush ahead of you along the channel and settle again fifty metres on.

How to identify one

Black and white, with a white forecrown, white-spotted black mantle, and a long deeply forked tail banded in white that it fans and pumps constantly. Slaty-backed Forktail is plain slate above with no spotting. Little Forktail is much smaller, with a short tail and a restless bobbing action.

Common questions

When is the best time to see a Spotted Forktail near Ranikhet?
Year-round, but winter is easiest — birds move down to streams around 1,400–1,900m and are far less scattered. Early morning and late afternoon, when they feed along the wet boulders. Unlike the pheasants there is no narrow window, which makes this one of the most dependable good birds on the route.
Where exactly do you find them?
Fast, shaded streams with exposed boulders and leaf litter at the edges. The Chaubatia stream below the orchards is the reliable spot near Ranikhet, and the Binsar stream crossings hold a pair or two. Walk the water, not the ridge.
How do you tell it from a Slaty-backed Forktail?
The Spotted has a white-spotted black mantle and a longer, deeply forked tail with broader white bands. Slaty-backed is plain slate above with no spotting. Little Forktail is much smaller with a short, barely forked tail and bobs constantly.
Is it a good bird for photography?
One of the best on the route. It works a predictable stretch of stream, comes back to the same boulders, and tolerates a still observer. The difficulty is light — deep shade and high contrast between black and white plumage.
