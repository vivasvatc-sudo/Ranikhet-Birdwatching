Ranikhet: the pheasant ridges

Based at Firwood Cottage in Majkhali, ten minutes from Ranikhet, walking the ground I know best. Dawn starts at 05:45, 3–5km on graded orchard track and ridge path between 1,700 and 2,050m, back by mid-morning. Afternoons are yours.

Three or four days, 80–100 species, and the one place on the trip where Cheer Pheasant is a real prospect. No camping, nothing above 2,100m, and a vehicle within reach all day.

The pheasants

Ranikhet is one of the few reliable places left for Cheer Pheasant

It is the reason to come here rather than anywhere else in the Himalaya, and the reason this is the tour I'd book first. Here is where I'd set expectations, in order.

Cheer Pheasant Catreus wallichii VULNERABLE
Why people come to Ranikhet

Global range is a thin band of the western Himalaya, and the grass-and-scrub slopes around Ranikhet hold one of the few dependable populations. It is the one bird I will not oversell: the dawn call carries a long way, the sighting is often brief, and some mornings give you nothing. We look for it on one morning of a three-day trip, and get a second attempt on the four.

Koklass Pheasant
Pucrasia macrolopha

Heard at dawn far more often than seen. Calling from the ridges above Majkhali most mornings in spring.

Kalij Pheasant
Lophura leucomelanos

The easy one. On the forest road at dusk, often a family party crossing in front of the vehicle.

Brown-fronted Woodpecker
Dendrocopos auriceps

On the lichened pines along every track we walk. Usually the first woodpecker of the trip, often with Himalayan Woodpecker nearby.

Owlets, two kinds
Glaucidium cuculoides · G. brodiei

Asian Barred and Collared Owlet both call through the afternoon from the pines, and both answer a whistled imitation. The reason the optional evening walk is worth doing.

Steppe Eagle
Aquila nipalensis

A winter and passage bird here, drifting along the ridge line on a warm afternoon. Numbers build from November.

The oak-forest supporting cast
Mixed flocks, all day

Himalayan Black-lored Tit (above) · Rock Bunting · White-tailed Nuthatch · Great Barbet · Rufous Sibia · Black-headed Jay.

DAY 1
Arrive Ranikhet

I meet you at Kathgodam station or Pantnagar airport — exact meeting point and my phone number are sent a week ahead. Road transfer of 3 to 4 hours with a stop at the Kosi barrage if the light holds. Evening: kit check and a look at the plan.

DAY 2
Ranikhet OBT eBird hotspot ↗

05:45 start, ten minutes by road, then 4km on level orchard track at 1,900m. Laughingthrushes, Rufous Sibia, Black-headed Jay, Himalayan Woodpecker, first Kalij. Back by 10:00. Optional short evening walk for owlets.

DAY 3
The Cheer ridge, pre-dawn

The early one: out at 05:15, a short drive, then up onto the grass slopes to be in position before the light. Cheer Pheasant call carries a long way before dawn. Koklass on the walk down, and the rest of the morning on the Chaubatia stream for forktails and redstarts. Afternoon off.

DAY 4
Majkhali ridge, then departure

A last morning on the shepherd paths above the cottage — mixed flocks, and raptors overhead late — then the transfer out. I plan the return so you are not making a same-night international connection out of Delhi.

Combining with Corbett

Most people flying in for Kumaon do Corbett too, and they should — it's a different bird list entirely. I don't run trips inside the park: it needs park-licensed naturalists, quota permits and safari slots booked months out, and you're better served by someone who does that every week. What I can do is make the two halves fit.

HILLS FIRST
Ranikhet → Corbett

The way I'd do it. Altitude birds while you're fresh, then drop to the terai. 3.5 hours by road, and I can stop you at the Kosi on the way down.

PARK FIRST
Corbett → Ranikhet

Works if your Dhikala dates are fixed and the hills have to fit around them. Same transfer, and you finish somewhere quiet.

MINIMUM
3 + 3 nights

Less than three in the hills and you spend most of it travelling. Ten days total is where the trip stops feeling rushed.

Tell me at enquiry that Corbett is in the plan and I'll send the operators I'd use myself, along with the dates that leave enough margin between the two halves. No commission, no markup — I just want your hill days to work.

Before you ask

Can I see a Himalayan Monal on this trip?
Not reliably, and I would rather say so now. The dependable Monal ground sits higher and further out than this route goes. If Monal is the reason you are coming, tell me at enquiry — I will either build a longer trip that reaches it, or point you to someone better placed for it.
Where do we stay?
On the Ranikhet tour, at Firwood Cottage in Majkhali — simple, clean, hot water, heated in winter. On the Binsar tour, at the forest rest house inside the sanctuary, which is basic on purpose: being inside at first light is the whole point. I will send photographs of either before you commit.
Is it suitable for a photographer?
Partly. The forest is dark and this is a walking trip at a birding pace, so expect record shots rather than portfolio images. If photography is the point, say so and I will slow the route down and build in longer sits.
When do I pay, and what if I cancel?
20% deposit to confirm, balance 30 days before arrival. Full refund of the deposit up to 45 days out, 50% up to 21 days. Bank transfer or UPI; I can invoice in USD, GBP or INR.
What should I bring?
Binoculars, layers for 4°C at dawn in winter, boots with grip for wet track, and a torch for the rest house. I send a full list with the itinerary. Scopes are useful but not essential on this route.
