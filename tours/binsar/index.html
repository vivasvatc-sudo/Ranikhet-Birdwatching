Binsar: inside the sanctuary

Two nights at 2,412m inside Binsar Wildlife Sanctuary, at the forest rest house. This one is about the place more than the list — unbroken oak and rhododendron, no traffic, no village noise, and snow peaks along the horizon on a clear morning.

You walk out of the door into forest, which no lodge outside the gate can offer. Around 6km a day with one real climb, and the rest house is basic on purpose — being inside at first light is the whole point.

The sanctuary

Binsar is about walking in true Himalayan sanctuary wilderness

Come here for the forest rather than for a target bird. Binsar is an Important Bird Area with 173 recorded species, but the reason to spend the nights inside is that there is nothing else inside — no traffic, no village, no other groups at dawn. The birds are what happens while you are standing in it.

What the two nights actually buy

Unbroken oak and rhododendron from the door of the rest house, first light without a drive, and the Zero Point ridge with snow peaks along the horizon on a clear morning. Nobody staying outside the gate gets any of that. In exchange the rest house is basic — cold water some mornings, simple food, no phone signal worth the name.

Koklass Pheasant
Pucrasia macrolopha

Heard at dawn far more often than seen. Calling from the ridge above Zero Point most mornings in spring.

Kalij Pheasant
Lophura leucomelanos

The easy one. On the forest road at dusk, often a family party crossing in front of the vehicle.

Eurasian Jay
Garrulus glandarius

Localised here and wanted by most visiting birders. Oak canopy, usually alongside Black-headed Jay.

Himalayan Griffon
Gyps himalayensis

Late morning over the ridges once the thermals build. Best watched from the Zero Point viewpoint.

Steppe Eagle
Aquila nipalensis

On passage and through the winter, using the same thermals as the griffons. The Zero Point ridge is the place to sit and wait.

Asian Barred Owlet
Glaucidium cuculoides

Daytime calling from the oaks around the rest house, and the easiest owl to actually see rather than hear.

The oak-forest supporting cast
Mixed flocks, all day

White-tailed Nuthatch · Black-throated Sunbird · Great Barbet · Rufous Sibia · Yellow-bellied Fantail · Spotted Forktail.

Most people choose this

The rest house is basic and beds inside the sanctuary are limited, so Binsar dates need more notice than Ranikhet ones — a month at least.

DAY 1
Up to the sanctuary, 2,412m

I meet you at Kathgodam, Pantnagar or in Ranikhet — exact meeting point and my phone number sent a week ahead. Transfer up through the oak belt, into the gate, and settled at the forest rest house by afternoon. Late walk on the ridge track for Kalij on the road at dusk.

DAY 2
Zero Point, and the day in the forest

Out of the door at first light — no drive. Koklass calling from above, mixed flocks through the oaks, and the ridge for snow peaks and griffons once the thermals build. Afternoon down into the stream gullies if the weather holds.

DAY 3
Last dawn, then down

A final morning at the rest-house clearing and on the track, then the transfer out. I plan the return so you are not making a same-night international connection out of Delhi.

Combining with Corbett

Most people flying in for Kumaon do Corbett too, and they should — it's a different bird list entirely. I don't run trips inside the park: it needs park-licensed naturalists, quota permits and safari slots booked months out, and you're better served by someone who does that every week. What I can do is make the two halves fit.

HILLS FIRST
Ranikhet → Corbett

The way I'd do it. Altitude birds while you're fresh, then drop to the terai. 3.5 hours by road, and I can stop you at the Kosi on the way down.

PARK FIRST
Corbett → Ranikhet

Works if your Dhikala dates are fixed and the hills have to fit around them. Same transfer, and you finish somewhere quiet.

MINIMUM
3 + 3 nights

Less than three in the hills and you spend most of it travelling. Ten days total is where the trip stops feeling rushed.

Tell me at enquiry that Corbett is in the plan and I'll send the operators I'd use myself, along with the dates that leave enough margin between the two halves. No commission, no markup — I just want your hill days to work.

Before you ask

Can I see a Himalayan Monal on this trip?
Not reliably, and I would rather say so now. The dependable Monal ground sits higher and further out than this route goes. If Monal is the reason you are coming, tell me at enquiry — I will either build a longer trip that reaches it, or point you to someone better placed for it.
Where do we stay?
On the Ranikhet tour, at Firwood Cottage in Majkhali — simple, clean, hot water, heated in winter. On the Binsar tour, at the forest rest house inside the sanctuary, which is basic on purpose: being inside at first light is the whole point. I will send photographs of either before you commit.
Is it suitable for a photographer?
Partly. The forest is dark and this is a walking trip at a birding pace, so expect record shots rather than portfolio images. If photography is the point, say so and I will slow the route down and build in longer sits.
When do I pay, and what if I cancel?
20% deposit to confirm, balance 30 days before arrival. Full refund of the deposit up to 45 days out, 50% up to 21 days. Bank transfer or UPI; I can invoice in USD, GBP or INR.
What should I bring?
Binoculars, layers for 4°C at dawn in winter, boots with grip for wet track, and a torch for the rest house. I send a full list with the itinerary. Scopes are useful but not essential on this route.
